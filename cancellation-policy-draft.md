# Cancellation policy

## Cancelling

| Season | Notice needed | You keep | With less notice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal dates | more than 60 days | 20% | no refund |
| Easter & Thanksgiving | more than 90 days | 30% | no refund |
| Christmas & New Year | more than 120 days | 30% | no refund |

So for a guest booked in for 1 May:

- They cancel on 1 February, 89 days ahead. You keep $3,640 and send back the
  rest.
- They cancel on 15 April, 16 days ahead. You keep everything.

The notice period is the part guests get wrong. Two people both cancel 100 days
ahead — the one booked for June gets a refund, the one booked for Christmas
doesn't, because Christmas needs 120 days.

## Moving dates is free

No fee, and everything they've paid moves across to the new dates. The new week
just has to be within 12 months of the original, at least 7 nights, and free.

If they move into a more expensive week they pay the difference, but never a
change fee on top.

## Never refunded

- Guests who don't turn up
- Guests who leave early — the unused nights aren't refunded
- Anything booked but not used, like an excursion they skipped

## Refunds

Money goes back to the card they paid with and takes 5–10 days to arrive. Worth
telling guests, so they don't chase you after two days.

## Two things on your side

**Refunds are done by hand.** Stripe has no setting for cancellation
percentages — it's a card processor, not a booking system. What it gives you is
a Refund button on each payment, where you type the amount. On an $18,200
booking that means:

| | You send back | You keep |
|---|---|---|
| Normal dates, cancelled in time | $14,560 | $3,640 |
| Holiday dates, cancelled in time | $12,740 | $5,460 |
| Cancelled too late | nothing | everything |

**Your Stripe name is in lower case.** The payment page currently reads "Pay
securely at seaview mirage villa" — it should match the website, Sea View Mirage
Villa. You can change it under Settings → Business → Public details.

Worth doing before real payments start. When someone doesn't recognise a charge
on their statement, they usually report it as fraud.
