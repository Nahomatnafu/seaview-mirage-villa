# Cancellation policy — please check these numbers

You said to use whatever makes sense, so we've chosen the numbers and written
the policy. **It's on the website now.**

Below is every number with a real example. You don't need to reply point by
point — just tell us if any example looks wrong to you.

All examples use a normal 7-night booking: **$2,600 a night = $18,200 total**,
paid as $4,550 + $6,370 + $7,280.

---

## 1. Standard dates — 60 days, then 20%

**The rule:** if a guest cancels **more than 60 days** before they arrive, they
get their money back **minus 20%**. Inside 60 days, they get nothing back.

> **Example.** A guest books for 1 May and has paid $10,920 so far.
>
> - They cancel on **1 February** (89 days ahead) → you keep 20% of $18,200 =
>   **$3,640**. You send back **$7,280**.
> - They cancel on **15 April** (16 days ahead) → **you keep everything.** No
>   refund.

**Why 20%?** Your notes said 5% in one place and 20% in another. We took 20%
because your deposit is 25% — so the fee is always smaller than what they've
already paid you. You never have to chase anyone for money.

*If you'd rather be more generous, 5% is fine — that would be $910 instead of
$3,640 on the example above. Just say so.*

---

## 2. Holiday dates — 30% instead of 20%

**The rule:** same idea, bigger fee, because holiday weeks are harder to re-sell.

> **Example.** Same $18,200 booking, but over Christmas. Guest cancels in good
> time → you keep 30% = **$5,460**, and send back **$12,740**.

This is the number you already confirmed. We've applied it to **Easter,
Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year** — you said "holiday", so we treated them
all the same.

---

## 3. How much notice for each season

Holiday weeks need more notice, because if someone drops out you have less time
to fill the week.

| Season | Cancel earlier than this → refund (less the fee) | Cancel later → no refund |
|---|---|---|
| Normal dates | more than **60 days** ahead | 60 days or less |
| Easter & Thanksgiving | more than **90 days** ahead | 90 days or less |
| Christmas & New Year | more than **120 days** ahead | 120 days or less |

> **Example.** Two guests both cancel **100 days** before arrival.
>
> - Guest A booked a **normal week in June** → 100 days is plenty of notice.
>   Refunded, less 20%.
> - Guest B booked **Christmas week** → 100 days is *not* enough (needs 120).
>   **No refund.**

These are the deadlines you gave us originally. Unchanged.

---

## 4. Moving dates — free

**The rule:** no fee, ever. Everything they've paid moves to the new dates.

> **Example.** A guest has paid $10,920 for a week in May and asks to move to
> September. All $10,920 moves across. They pay nothing extra to change.

Three conditions:

- The new dates must be **within 12 months** of their original arrival date.
- Still a **minimum 7 nights**.
- The week has to be **free**.

> **Example.** They move from a normal week to Christmas week, and Christmas
> costs more. They pay the difference. They don't get charged a change fee on
> top.

**Why 12 months?** You said you'd always find them another week, which is now
the policy. The 12-month limit just stops someone holding a credit forever — a
guest who paid in 2026 and still hasn't travelled by 2029 is a problem with no
clean answer.

---

## 5. What's never refunded

Regardless of notice:

- Someone who **doesn't turn up**.
- Someone who **leaves early** — no refund for the unused nights.
- Anything **booked but not used** (an excursion they skipped, a spa treatment
  they didn't take).

> **Example.** A guest books 10 nights, leaves after 6 because of a family
> emergency. You keep the full amount. Sad, but it's the rule everywhere.

---

## 6. Refunds take 5–10 business days

Money goes back to the same card they paid with. It isn't instant — banks take
about a week. Worth saying up front so nobody chases you on day two.

## Two things you should know

### Stripe can't do the percentage for you

You mentioned setting the return percentage in Stripe. **Stripe has no such
setting** — it's a card processor, not a booking system. What it gives you is a
**Refund** button on each payment, where you type the amount.

So the percentages live on the website (that's the part we just published), and
you type the number in when someone cancels. Here's the cheat sheet for a
$18,200 booking:

| Situation | You send back | You keep |
|---|---|---|
| Normal dates, cancelled in time | $14,560 | $3,640 |
| Holiday dates, cancelled in time | $12,740 | $5,460 |
| Cancelled too late | $0 | everything |
| Moving dates | nothing changes hands | — |

### Your Stripe name shows in lower case

On the payment page it currently reads *"Pay securely at seaview mirage
villa"*. It should say **Sea View Mirage Villa**, like the website.

Fix it in Stripe under **Settings → Business → Public details**. Worth doing
before real money starts moving — when someone doesn't recognise a charge on
their statement, the usual reaction is to report it as fraud.
