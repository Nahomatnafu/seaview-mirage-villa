### Why 20%

Your own notes said 5% in one place and 20% in another, so we took 20%:

- Your deposit is 25%. A 20% fee sits just under it, so a guest who cancels
  never owes you more than they've already paid. Nothing to chase, no invoice
  to send.
- A cancellation 61 days out still costs you real money — staff scheduled, food
  ordered, and a week that's hard to fill at short notice.

**If you'd rather compete on being flexible, 5% is a fine choice** — just say so
and we'll change it. It means absorbing most of the loss yourself.

### Why 30% on all holidays, not just Christmas

You said "Holiday — 30%." We've applied that to Easter, Thanksgiving, Christmas
and New Year alike. Splitting them into different rates would be inventing a
distinction you never asked for.

### Why 12 months on moved dates

You said you'd always find another week, and there's no fee to move — that's
now the policy. The 12-month limit just stops a credit sitting open forever. A
guest who paid in 2026 and hasn't travelled by 2028 becomes a problem with no
clean answer.

## What the website now says

**Cancelling.** On standard dates, cancel 61 or more days before arrival and
you're refunded less a 20% fee. Cancel 60 days or fewer before arrival and no
refund is due. Easter and Thanksgiving use a 90-day deadline, Christmas and New
Year a 120-day deadline, both with a 30% fee.

No refund is given for services booked but not used, for guests who don't
arrive, or for late arrival or early departure once the stay has begun.

**Moving your dates.** No fee. Everything paid moves across. New dates must be
within 12 months of the original arrival date, must still meet the 7-night
minimum, and are subject to availability — if they fall in a higher-priced
season, the difference is payable. The original dates are then released.

**Refunds** go back to the card used to pay, within 5–10 business days.

## Two things you should know

**1. Refunds can't be set as a percentage in Stripe.** You mentioned planning to
set the return percentage there. Stripe has no such setting — it's a payment
processor, not a booking system. What it gives you is a **Refund** button on any
payment, where you type the amount.

So the percentage lives on the website (that's what we've just published), and
you apply it by hand when someone cancels. On a 7-night booking of $18,200:

| Guest cancels | You refund |
|---|---|
| Standard dates, 61+ days out | $14,560 (keep 20% = $3,640) |
| Holiday dates, in the window | $12,740 (keep 30% = $5,460) |
| Inside the deadline | Nothing |

**2. Your Stripe account name shows as "seaview mirage villa" in lower case.**
Guests see it on the payment page — it currently reads *"Pay securely at seaview
mirage villa"*. It should be **Sea View Mirage Villa**, matching the website.

You can fix it in Stripe under **Settings → Business → Public details**. Worth
doing before real payments start: a charge a guest doesn't recognise is the most
common reason people report a payment as fraud.
